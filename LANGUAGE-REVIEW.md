# Journey to Net Zero: language review

Who this is for. Players in Malaysia and South East Asia who read English
well but did not grow up inside British or American English. The bar is a
secondary school student, playing without help, on a phone, with forty
seconds on the clock.

What was reviewed. Every string a player or facilitator can read: the phone,
the projector, the role briefs, the option cards, the crisis texts, the
headlines, the goals, the tips, the how-to-play sheet, the written guide and
the facilitator's script. Counts below come from a script run over the
source and the content pack, not from a sample.

`FLOW-REVIEW.md` covers sequencing. This document covers words only.

---

## 1. The verdict

The sentences are short. The words are not plain.

By the usual reading-level measures this copy looks easy: most sentences are
under ten words and few words are long. Those measures miss what actually
stops a non-native reader, and this copy has all of it:

1. **Idiom and metaphor.** "Hold the line", "belt-tightening", "bet the
   company", "cross the floor", "plug the hole", "a white flag on the gate",
   "lives over ledgers", "the long game", "cut out the middleman". A reader
   who does not already know the phrase cannot work it out from the words.
2. **Irony and understatement.** "Exporters are quietly delighted." "Painful,
   honest, correct." "Available, legal, filthy." "Money now. Questions
   never." "Slower. Cheaper. Actually fine." Irony is the last thing a
   second-language reader learns to hear. Read literally, half of these say
   the opposite of what they mean.
3. **Fragments with no subject and no verb.** 29 percent of option-card
   sentences are three words or fewer. "Rescue the giants. Cut everything
   else." "Four people. Four conversations." "Loud, dirty, available on
   Tuesday." The reader has to supply the missing half of the sentence, and
   the card gives them forty seconds to do it.
4. **Two or three names for the same thing.** The Reveal is also the
   Reckoning. The Talk is also the Table. A tip off is also an insider tip.
   A secret win is also a private goal and a secret goal. Money is Budget,
   fiscal, Company Money, capital, and just "money". Saying no is a veto,
   the Public Mandate, "the public says no", and "say no". A first-time
   reader assumes two names mean two things.
5. **Game words that are never defined where they are used.** Spotlight,
   veto, Public Trust, Clean Economy, "moves too", "the cheap card",
   "escalate", "Collaborate card", "Mt", "net", "partnership", "co-fund",
   "coalition". All defined in a 4,000-word guide on another page.
6. **Negatives stacked on negatives.** "Nothing makes you keep it." "Nobody
   is told who." "If they never do theirs, nothing is held against you."
   "Not close on at least one." Each is correct English and each takes two
   readings.
7. **Specialist news vocabulary on cards.** Effluent plume, dioxin, class
   action, plaintiffs, lender of last resort, conditionality, distributional
   study, inelastic, oversubscribed, behind-the-meter, demand response,
   mothballed, concession, catchment, transboundary. Fine in a newspaper.
   Not on a card a fourteen-year-old has to compare with two others.
8. **A voice written for a British audience.** "Ladies and gentlemen." "Give
   yourselves a hand." "That is not a hint. That is the entire design." "Four
   rational actors, one dead country." The tone is clever and dry. Clever and
   dry does not travel.

None of this needs the game to change. It needs one style, one vocabulary,
and every sentence rewritten to say the literal thing.

## 3. The eight patterns, with examples and fixes

Every example below is quoted from the current build. Every fix is written
to the standard in section 4.

### 3.1 Idiom and metaphor

| Now | Why it fails | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| Hold the Line. No new spending. Protect the reserves. | "Hold the line" is a military and telephone idiom. | Spend Nothing New. Keep the money we have. |
| Rescue for the giants, belt-tightening for the rest | Nobody tightens a belt here. | Big companies get a rescue. Everyone else gets cuts. |
| Being the boss who moved early and got it wrong. | "Moved" as "acted" is business idiom. | Being the boss who went clean too early and lost money. |
| Four MPs cross the floor at 11pm. | British parliament idiom. In Malaysia this is "party-hopping". | Four MPs change party at 11pm. |
| Sell the stake in NuriTenaga and plug the hole. | Two idioms in one line. | Sell the government's share of NuriTenaga to pay the bills. |
| A white flag on the gate, and a neighbour who answers. | A reference to a real Malaysian campaign, but only readable if you remember it. | Families who need food hang a white flag. Neighbours bring food. |
| Everything stops. Lives over ledgers. | "Ledgers" is an accounting word used as a metaphor. | Everything stops. People first, money second. |
| Get Out the Youth Vote | American campaign phrase. | Get Young People to Vote |
| Buy in bulk, cut out the middleman. | Idiom. | Buy in bulk, straight from the farm. |
| Take the seat. Shape it from inside. | Two metaphors. | Join the committee. Change the plan from inside. |
| It hits whoever takes the dirtiest card. | "Hits", "takes", "dirtiest" are all figurative. | It punishes the player who picks the card that pollutes most. |
| Sitting on a true warning costs you nothing in the rules. | "Sitting on" is idiom. | Keeping the warning to yourself costs you nothing in the rules. |
| Nobody loses a job on our watch. | Idiom. | We will not cut any jobs. |
| The season is worth more than the argument. | Three meanings of "season" and an abstract "argument". | Tourist money matters more than the science. |
| A card lands. A Spotlight fires. A promise bites. | The whole game runs on these three verbs, and none is literal. | A card works. A Spotlight is used. A promise is checked. |

| Now | Read literally by a second-language reader | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| Exporters are quietly delighted. | Exporters are happy, and that is good. | Exporters make more money. Everyone else pays more. |
| Painful, honest, correct. | This is the right answer. (It is meant as "right, but people will hate it".) | Fair, but people will be angry. |
| Available, legal, filthy. | A list of three facts. | Cheap and legal. Very dirty. |
| Money now. Questions never. | A promise of money. | Pay people now. Do not ask what went wrong. |
| Slower. Cheaper. Actually fine. | A neutral description. | Slower and cheaper than driving. It works. |
| The old way. It works. | Approval. | Give contracts to friends. It has always worked. |
| Not our problem. | A statement of fact. | Make customers pay more. |
| Somebody usually gasps. | Somebody is hurt. | People are often surprised. |
| Any resemblance to countries you know is entirely deliberate. | A legal notice. | This country is made up. It is a lot like real ones on purpose. |
| Give yourselves a hand. | Hold out your hand. | Well done. Clap for yourselves. |
| You got exactly what you wanted. The country is 3.2°C warmer. Was it worth it? | The tone reads as mockery. | You reached your own goal. The country did not reach its targets. Think about that. |

### 3.3 Fragments

A fragment asks the reader to guess the missing words. Give them the words.

| Now | Plain |
|---|---|
| Rescue the giants. Cut everything else. | Give money to the big companies. Cut spending on everything else. |
| Four people. Four conversations. | Talk to the four rebel MPs one by one. |
| Loud, dirty, available on Tuesday. | Diesel generators are loud and dirty, but you can buy them today. |
| Twelve thousand redundancies. Offshore the rest. | Cut 12,000 jobs. Move the other jobs to another country. |
| Everyone indoors. For months. | Everyone stays home for months. |
| Nine weeks. Every plant. | Every factory cuts its water use in half in nine weeks. |
| Tents until they commit. | Camp outside Parliament until they agree. |
| Buckets, tankers, and six days of queuing. | People queue for water from tankers for six days. |
| Masks, oxygen, ventilators. At cost. | Make masks, oxygen and ventilators, and sell them at no profit. |
| A strongly worded note. Nothing else. | Send an angry letter to the other country. Do nothing else. |

Headlines may stay as headlines. A headline is a known form in Malaysia and
readers expect it to be short. Even so, a headline should be a plain
statement of what happened, not a joke: "Rescue for the giants,
belt-tightening for the rest" becomes "Big exporters rescued. Everyone else
faces cuts."

| Now | Plain, same warmth |
|---|---|

## 4. The plain English standard for this game

Ten rules. Every string on every surface is checked against them.

1. **One idea per sentence. Subject, verb, object.** No fragments in
   instructions, cards or briefs. Headlines may be short, but they still
   state a fact.
2. **Phone sentences are 12 words or fewer.** Projector sentences are 15 or
   fewer. Longer is allowed only in the written guide.
3. **Say the literal thing.** No idiom, no metaphor, no irony, no sarcasm,
   no understatement. If a sentence could be read two ways, rewrite it.
4. **One name per thing.** The list in 3.4 is the vocabulary. Nothing else
   is used for those things, on any surface, in the script or in the code.
5. **Define a game word on the screen where it appears.** One line, under
   the word, every time. The guide is a reference, not the place a player
   learns the word.
6. **Verbs a child knows.** Pick, choose, lock, send, ask, promise, say no,
   share, keep, wait, look up, talk. Not: escalate, collaborate, co-fund,
   pledge, seal, commit, resolve, fire, land, bite.
7. **Prefer the positive.** "You can change it until you lock" rather than
   "Nothing is final until you lock."
8. **Numbers with their unit in words, the first time on any screen.** "100
   million tonnes of carbon", then "100" on the meter.
9. **Malaysian English is the target, not British or American.** "Can" and
   "cannot" are strong and welcome. "Lorry", "petrol", "kampung", "hawker",
   "condo" are the right words. "Party-hopping", not "crossing the floor".
   "Handphone" is fine. Avoid "gotten", "fall", "sidewalk", "lobby" as a
   verb.
10. **Character lines may have personality. They may not have idiom.** "I
    can fund that. What do I tell the people who voted for me?" is a good
    line and needs no change. "Money is no object" does.

Reading target: a Malaysian Form 2 student, or CEFR B1, reads every phone
screen once and knows what to do.

### 5.3 The briefing (projector)

Now:

> Three targets. All three, or nobody wins. Six crises. Each one goes the
> same way. You talk, you each pick a card in secret, then all four turn over
> at once. Nothing you promise is binding. You each hold a secret win of your
> own. It counts for nothing unless the country hits all three targets.

Plain:

> Three targets. You must reach all three, or nobody wins. There are six
> crises. Each one works the same way. You talk. You each pick a card in
> secret. Then all four cards are shown at once. Promises are not enforced.
> You each have a secret goal too. It only counts if the country reaches all
> three targets.

Target lines: "CARBON · Down to net zero · 100 million tonnes today",
"ECONOMY · Growing 5% a year on average · 4.5% today", "QUALITY OF LIFE ·
Up to 7 out of 10 · 6.0 today".

### 5.13 The 216 option cards

The titles are mostly fine. The one-line descriptions are where the idiom,
irony and fragments concentrate: 39 idiom hits and 148 fragments across 216
cards. A full rewrite is a content-pack task, not a code task, and should be
done in one pass against the standard. The rules for that pass:

1. Every description is one or two full sentences with a subject and a verb.
2. The description says what the card does to the country, not how it
   feels. "Available, legal, filthy" becomes "Cheap and legal. Very dirty."
3. No irony. If the card is a bad idea, the arrows say so. The words do not
   need to sneer.
4. No specialist word without a plain one beside it.
5. Titles use verbs a child knows: Build, Spend, Cut, Pay, Sell, Ban, Help,
   Protest, Join, Publish. Not: Broker, Extract, Negotiate, Litigate,
   Localise, Reprice.

## 6. How to make it stick

1. **One vocabulary file.** Put the terms from 3.4 and the definitions from
   3.5 in one module, and build every label, chip and coach line from it.
   Today the same term is typed by hand in eleven files and the content pack.
2. **A banned-words test.** A unit test that runs over every string in the
   source and the content pack and fails on the idioms in section 3 and on
   the second names in 3.4. The repository already tests that no number
   leaks to a phone. This is the same idea for words.
3. **A sentence-length test.** Fail any phone string over 12 words and any
   projector string over 15, with an allow list for the guide.
4. **Rewrite the content pack in one pass**, against section 5.13, by one
   writer, and have two Malaysian secondary school students read twenty
   cards each and say what the card does. If they cannot, the card is not
   done.
5. **Bahasa Malaysia comes after this, not instead of it.** A plain English
   pack translates cleanly. An idiomatic one does not translate at all.
