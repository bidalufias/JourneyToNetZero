1 · The country

You are running the Republic of Semenanjara. It doesn't exist. It could.

Thirty-four million people on a tropical peninsula. Monsoon rains from November. Palm and rubber plantations inland, chemical parks and semiconductor factories on the west coast, gas fields offshore, tourist islands to the east. The capital is Kota Damai. The currency is the Ringga. The river that gives four million people their drinking water is the Sungai Jernih.

Semenanjara has grown fast for thirty years. It is now middle-income, young, impatient and online. It also burns a great deal of fossil fuel, floods every year, and has an economy built on exactly the industries the world is trying to move away from.

The year is 2025. You have until 2050. In game terms, that's six rounds and about thirty minutes.

2 · Your mission

All four of you share one mission. There are three numbers, and you must hit all three. Two out of three is a failure.

🌍 Emissions300 → 200 or below

How much greenhouse gas the country puts out, measured in million tonnes. Today it's 300. You need to cut 100. Think of it as the country's exhaust pipe.

📈 Economic growth5% average

How fast the economy is growing. Today it's 4.5%. You need the average across all six rounds to be 5% or better. A bad round early has to be made up later. Growth means jobs, wages and tax money, and this is not optional.

😊 Happiness6.0 → 7.0 or above

How good life actually is here, scored out of 10, the same idea as the World Happiness Report. Today it's 6.0. Air, water, jobs, prices, safety, trust. A country can hit its climate target and still be miserable. That counts as losing.

The point of three numbers

Any one of these is easy on its own. You could crash the economy and emissions would fall. You could burn coal and growth would soar. The game is the trade-off between them, and no single player controls more than a slice of any one number.

3 · The hidden number

There is a fourth meter on the big screen: Green Economy Share. It starts at 10%. Nobody will tell you how important it is. It is the most important thing in the game.

Here is why. Growth creates emissions. Every round, the economy grows, and that growth automatically adds emissions, whatever anyone chooses. You can be doing everything right and still watch the number climb.

Green Economy Share is how much of the economy runs on clean things: renewable power, efficient transport, restored land, clean manufacturing. The higher it goes, the less carbon each unit of growth produces.

Green Economy ShareWhat growth costs you in carbon
Under 20%Brutal. Every bit of growth adds a lot of emissions
21% to 40%Heavy
41% to 60%Manageable
Over 60%Light. You can grow and still cut

And there's a bonus: a bigger green economy also raises growth and raises happiness, because it's the new growth sector and because people prefer living in a clean, working country.

So the real answer to "how do we cut 100 million tonnes without wrecking the economy?" is not do less. It's build a different economy. Most tables work this out around Round 3. The earlier you work it out, the better your Round 6 will be.

4 · Who you are

Four seats. Each of you gets one. Read your own character properly, because you're going to be them for half an hour, and the game is far better if you actually argue like they would.

🏛 The Government

The Honourable Minister

52 · Minister for Energy, Environment and Climate

Who you are

Twenty-four years in the civil service before you went into politics. You represent Kuala Jernih, a semi-rural seat where half your voters work in plantations and the other half commute into the city. People call you competent and slightly boring. You take that as a compliment.

What you believe

The transition has to happen and you are the only person in this room who can actually fund it. You also believe that a government that loses an election achieves nothing at all, so staying in power is not vanity. It's the job.

What you're afraid of

Being remembered as the minister who made petrol expensive.

Your money: Fiscal Points

You start with 4. From Round 2 you get +2 every round, and +1 more if the economy is doing well. The big moves cost 2 or 3, so sometimes you save a round to afford the thing that matters. You can also give a point away to help someone else act.

How to play it

You move faster than anyone else here. You can spend, and you can make law. But everything you do is paid for by someone who votes. Never give something away without asking what you get back.

Things you might say

  • I can fund that. What do I tell the people in Perindu?
  • Give me a public reason to spend this and I'll spend it.
  • If I do that I lose the seat, and then none of this happens.
You'd never say: "Money's no object."
🏭 The Business

The Business Tycoon

58 · Group CEO, Sawit Prima: palm, property, power

Who you are

Third generation. You took over at 41, the week your father died. Forty thousand employees. You have read every major climate report of the last decade and can quote them, which surprises people who assume you haven't.

What you believe

You will go green the moment it is cheaper than not going green, or the moment somebody makes staying dirty more expensive. You don't think that makes you a villain. You think it makes you honest, and you're quietly irritated by people who pretend otherwise.

What you're afraid of

Being the chief executive who bet the company early and got it wrong.

Your money: Capital

You start with 5. You gain +1 every round, and +1 more when the economy is growing well. Real change costs you 3, which is a serious bite. Partnership options cost only 1, but they only work if somebody else chips in.

How to play it

You own the emissions. You also own the jobs, the exports and most of the tax base. That gives you leverage. Use it. Never volunteer to pay for something alone if you can get it co-funded.

Things you might say

  • I'll do it, if the Government shares the cost.
  • That's a ten-year investment and I'm judged every quarter.
  • You want me to cut thirty percent? Fine. Who pays for the retraining?
You'd never say: "Profit doesn't matter here."
🏘 The Community

The Community Elder

47 · Food stall owner, chair of the residents' association

Who you are

You run a stall in Kampung Baru Jernih and you chair the residents' association, which means you speak for thirty-four million people who never elected you but definitely agree with you. Your stall has flooded three times in eight years. Your son has asthma. Your electricity bill doubled last year.

What you believe

Clean air and affordable petrol are both perfectly reasonable things to want, and you are tired of being told to pick one. You've heard a lot of promises. You remember all of them.

What you're afraid of

Being asked to sacrifice again by people who won't have to.

Your power: Trust and the Public Mandate

You have no money and no laws. You have something better. Every round you award 2 Trust tokens: one to whoever looked after people best, one to whoever did most for the future. Trust is what the Government needs before it can be brave, and what the Business needs to keep operating.

You also hold 2 Public Mandate vetoes for the whole game. Spend one to take somebody's worst option off the table for a round. "The public will simply not accept this." Use them on the person who is wrecking it for everyone.

How to play it

Make them earn it out loud. Ask what things cost. Remind people what they promised last round. You are the only player who can say "no" and make it stick.

Things you might say

  • Explain that to me like I have to pay for it, because I do.
  • You promised us this last time. What happened?
  • We'll accept it. But you go first.
You'd never say: "Whatever you think is best."
✊ The Activist

The Youth Activist

26 · Founder, Bangkit Iklim (Climate Rise)

Who you are

Law degree, no job in law. You organised the first climate strike in Kota Damai when you were nineteen, and four hundred people came. Last year ninety thousand came. You have no budget, no staff worth the name, and the largest following in the country.

What you believe

The science isn't negotiable and the timeline isn't a preference. Everyone in this room will personally be fine whatever happens, and that is precisely the problem.

What you're afraid of

Becoming the person who sat in a nice room, signed something, and changed nothing.

Your power: 3 Spotlights, and your credibility

You can't build anything or fund anything. What you can do is make ignoring you more expensive than listening to you. A Spotlight names one player publicly before choices lock. If they then pick their dirtiest option, it only half works, they lose Trust, and the public actually feels better for the accountability.

You only get three, for the whole game. And every time you take the seat at the table and compromise, you become slightly less potent for the rest of the game. That is not a bug. That is the job.

How to play it

You have three real choices every round: escalate (loud, costly, effective against bad actors), collaborate (get real influence, spend a little of your soul), or build evidence (nothing happens now, something big happens later). Pick deliberately.

Things you might say

  • That's not a plan, that's a press release.
  • I'll back you publicly if you commit to it publicly.
  • Everyone in this room will be fine. That's the problem.
You'd never say: "Let's revisit this next year."

5 · Your secret goal

Before Round 1, your phone offers you three private goals. Pick one. Don't show anyone.

Everyone can read the full list below, so that part isn't secret. What's secret is which one you chose. You may lie about it freely. People will ask.

Some of these pull in the same direction as the country's mission. Some pull against it. That tension is the game.

🏛 Government

  • LandslideFinish holding 6 or more Trust tokens.
  • Steady HandGrowth never falls below 4.0% in any single round.
  • The LegacyGet Green Economy Share to 55% or higher.

🏭 Business

  • Market DominanceFinish with 6 or more Capital.
  • Green ChampionPersonally deliver 40 million tonnes of cuts or more.
  • Licence to OperateFinish with at least 2 Trust and at least 4 Capital.

🏘 Community

  • A Happy NationHappiness finishes at 8.0 or above.
  • Nobody Left BehindHappiness never drops below 6.3 in any round.
  • Our Own Two HandsLand 4 or more successful community-led actions.

✊ Activist

  • No CompromiseNever once take the collaborate option, and still finish at 175 million tonnes or below.
  • The Long GameGet Green Economy Share to 52% or higher.
  • Justice FirstHappiness finishes at 7.0+ and at least one of your Spotlights lands.
Read this twice

If the country misses even one of its three targets, nobody wins, including anyone who achieved their private goal. You'll get a card called Hollow Victory instead. Your secret goal is worth chasing, but not at the cost of the mission.

6 · How a round works

Six rounds. Four minutes each. Same shape every time, so you'll have it by Round 2.

0:30

The Crisis

The big screen reveals what's just happened: a market crash, a pandemic, a poisoned reservoir. Your phone buzzes with the same news plus one private line only you can see.

1:30

The Table

Talk. Argue, plead, threaten, bargain, form alliances, make promises. Send each other resources. This is the game. Put the phone down and use your mouth.

0:45

The Choice

Three options on your phone. Pick one, in secret. Everyone locks in at the same time. You do not have to do what you just promised.

1:15

The Reckoning

The four cards flip on the big screen, one at a time. The meters move. The headlines print. Somebody usually gasps. Look up for this bit.

Two things that make this game different

First, promises are not enforced. If you say you'll pick the green option and then don't, the game lets you. The big screen will announce it. The room will react. That moment is worth more than any lecture.

Second, early rounds decide late rounds. Build coal in Round 2 and the pollution disaster in Round 5 hits roughly twice as hard. Set up a citizens' river watch in Round 5 and the flood in Round 6 does noticeably less damage. Nothing you do is forgotten.

The one thing that makes everything easier

When three or four of you choose constructive options in the same round, the country gets a Coalition Bonus: an extra cut in emissions, a lift in happiness, and a jump in the green economy that no player can produce alone. Four aligned is dramatically better than three.

Holding someone to account counts as constructive. Regulating a polluter, or shining a spotlight on one, is cooperation, not conflict. The only things that break the coalition are choosing to expand dirtily, deregulating, or demanding that somebody else pay.

If you take one strategic idea into the room, take that one.

8 · Insider tips

Every round, exactly one player receives a private tip on their phone. Nobody is told who got it.

It might be an early warning about the next crisis. It might be something about another player's secret goal. It might be a leaked memo about a consequence heading your way. It might be a rumour that isn't true.

Each tip is labelled either CONFIRMED (always true) or UNVERIFIED (true about half the time).

You can keep it to yourself, share it honestly, or describe it inaccurately. Nobody can check. But you also have the option to Publish to the Nation, putting it on the big screen with your name on it. If it turns out true, you gain Trust. If it turns out false, you lose Trust.

Sitting on a true warning costs you nothing in the rules. It might cost you something else.

9 · Winning and losing

If the country hits all three targets

Everyone earns the title NATION BUILDER. Then the private goals are revealed one at a time, and anyone who also hit theirs collects a personal title on top. Nobody is embarrassed. Everyone takes something home.

If the country misses even one

Every private goal is revealed, including the ones people achieved. And anyone who hit theirs receives this:

🏆 HOLLOW VICTORY
You got exactly what you wanted. The country is 3.2°C warmer. Was it worth it?

There is no partial credit and no consolation prize. That's deliberate. It is also, more or less, how the actual thing works.

10 · Playing in character

You'll get much more out of this if you commit to the role. Four things help.

1 · Argue from your interests, not your opinions

You may personally think the Business should just decarbonise immediately. The Business Tycoon doesn't. Play them properly. Say the things they'd say, defend what they'd defend. The insight comes from inhabiting a position you don't hold.

2 · Every character here is right about something

The Honourable Minister genuinely can't act without votes. The Business Tycoon genuinely can't outrun their shareholders. The Community Elder genuinely can't absorb another price rise. The Youth Activist genuinely is running out of time. Nobody in this game is the idiot. If you find yourself thinking one of them is, you're not listening.

3 · Say things out loud

Don't just pick an option. Announce what you're minded to do and ask what it's worth. Deals only happen if somebody proposes one. The most valuable sentence in this game is: "What if we both did it?"

4 · You are allowed to break a promise

You are also allowed to be furious when someone breaks one on you. Both are part of the design. Just be ready to talk about it afterwards, because the facilitator will absolutely ask.

House rules
  • Phones face down during The Table. Look at each other.
  • Don't show anyone your secret goal, even at the end. Wait for the reveal.
  • Speak in character where you can. "I can't afford that" beats "the Government option costs two."
  • When the timer hits zero it locks whether you're ready or not. Decide early.

11 · Every word explained

Emissions / MtCO₂e
Greenhouse gas released, measured in million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent. "Equivalent" just means other gases are converted into the carbon dioxide that would cause the same warming, so it's all one number.
Net zero
Putting no more greenhouse gas into the air than you take out. This game doesn't ask you to reach it. It asks you to cut a third of the way there while keeping the country working.
GDP growth
How much bigger the economy got this year, as a percentage. More growth generally means more jobs, higher wages and more tax money for the government to spend.
Happiness score
A 0 to 10 measure of how good life actually is, the same idea the World Happiness Report uses. It covers income, health, clean air and water, safety, and whether people trust their institutions.
Green Economy Share
How much of the economy runs on clean industry. The higher it is, the less carbon each bit of growth produces, and the happier and faster-growing the country gets. See section 3.
Fiscal Points
The Government's money. Public spending capacity.
Capital
The Business's money. Investment capacity.
Trust
Public confidence, awarded by the Community, 2 tokens every round. It gates what the Government is allowed to attempt and protects the Business's right to operate.
Spotlight
The Activist's power to name someone publicly before choices lock. Three per game.
Public Mandate
The Community's power to remove somebody's worst option for one round. Two per game.
Coalition Bonus
Extra progress the country gets when three or four players choose constructive options in the same round. Cannot be produced by any player alone.
Just transition
Moving to a clean economy without dumping the cost on workers and poorer households. Comes up repeatedly, especially around the refinery town of Perindu.
Subsidy
Government money that keeps a price artificially low, which here means mostly fuel. Popular, expensive, and it mainly benefits whoever buys the most fuel.
Decoupling
Growing the economy without growing emissions alongside it. The thing Green Economy Share actually measures.
Legacy flag
A consequence your early choice locks in that changes what happens later. You won't see them listed. You'll see the effect.

That's all of it. Room is waiting.
