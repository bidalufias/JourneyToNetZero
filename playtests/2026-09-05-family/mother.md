## 2. What worked

- **Typing the code made the seats appear immediately** with two short lines each — I could read Aisyah's seat to her in one breath. "Read the two lines, then tap one." was exactly right.
- **The card-picking step is clear**: "Tap a card. Then tap LOCK MY CARD." The ✓ on the card, the greyed "TAP A CARD FIRST", then "You can still change your card." — I never doubted where I was.
- **"Look up."** on the phone at the right moments really did move my eyes to the TV.
- **The ⋯ panel** answered almost every question I had (what Public Trust is, what happens if you run out of time). As a teacher I liked "WORDS ON YOUR SCREEN".
- **The reveal's "BROKE THE PROMISE ✕ / BOTH KEPT THE DEAL ✓"** was the best moment. The kids understood instantly that the game remembers what you said. That is a real lesson about politics.
- **The phone result card** ("Cut carbon by 9.3 million tonnes… It worked as planned… You got the moving together bonus") told me exactly what my choice did. My money dropped 5 → 4, which proved Azman really paid half.
- The **secret goal** confirmation ("You cannot change this later") stopped me tapping carelessly.

## 3. What did not work

- **"HOW TO PLAY" on the join page did nothing.** I tapped it, it went red, no page opened. First thing I tried, first thing broken. I had to find the rules under ⋯ later.
- **The talk happens before you can see your cards.** During "THE TALK" the "I will pick…" menu shows only card *names*: "Buy Parts From Local Factories / Sell More Abroad / Build a Parts Factory Together" — no cost, no arrows, no "Dirty card" label. Everyone promised blind. Azman promised "Defend the Ringga", then saw it had "economy down two arrows" and switched; Aisyah promised "Switch to Local Food" and found "red arrows". Both got "BROKE THE PROMISE ✕" for promises made without information. Aisyah said "Not fair, I only promised because I couldn't see the arrows!" — I agree with her.
- **The deal sentence cannot say "if you pay half."** My seat's own scripted line is "I will do it if the Government pays half." but "I will pick… if you…" only offers "…also picks a good card." / "…does not pick a dirty card." I had to use a different sentence than the one the game told me to say.
- **Public Trust went to the promise-breaker.** TV: "PUBLIC TRUST HELD Government 2 · Business 0 · Activist 0". Azman broke his promise and got both points; Aisyah's phone said "Cut carbon by 11.1 million tonnes. The biggest carbon cut this round" and she got nothing — she isn't even on the list. Danial's tip point ("free Public Trust point for me") vanished. Three of us thought it was wrong; Danial said "This game is rigged for the Minister." "Nobody hands these out. The people give them…" does not explain *how*.
- **Practice powers looked used up.** Aisyah: "my VETOES says 1 now. It said practice does not count! Did I lose one for real??" Danial: "it says 2 LEFT at the bottom but top still says 3." They came back afterwards, but the screen said "This does not count" while the counters went down. Danial also said the Spotlight "never asked me WHO to spotlight".
- **The practice reveal was too fast.** Azman: "the TV went so fast, I didn't even see the cards." Danial: "I missed the reveal." The real reveal also drew cards one at a time — Danial's box was still empty when I read it out.
- **The ⋯ panel is far too long for Aisyah.** She said "Wah, so many words in here, I cannot read all this… Too long. I close it."
- **Waiting.** The talk in round 1 was 2 minutes; the kids had said everything in 40 seconds. "So boring, one minute more." Azman finally pressed NEXT.
- **"‹" is not useful.** The TV said "Tap ‹ to read this round again"; it only re-showed the news headline I already had.
- **The ⋯ panel says "Three cards, forty seconds"**, but the practice had **two** cards and the real round's clock started at **0:54**. Small, but a teacher notices.
- **Nobody asked me to press "I AM READY" — it just appeared** and the whole family waited on me while I was reading my two lines. A "3 of 4 ready, waiting for Suraya" nudge on my own phone would have been kinder than three people shouting.

## 4. Words I did not understand, or that Aisyah (11) / Danial (15) would not

Asked out loud by Aisyah: **"crises"**, **"Minister"**, **"Profit"**, **"vetoes"**, **"Economy 4.5%"**, **"Net Zero"**, **"enforced"**, **"Ringga"**, **"Imported"**, **"Exporters"**, **"Price Controls"**, **"Public Trust point"**, and (from me) **"subsidy"**.

Ones I think are hard for children and were never explained on screen:
- **"Partnership. The Government pays half. It works in full."** — "works in full" is odd English.
- **"It breaks moving together."** — "moving together" as a noun is not natural; I only understood after the ⋯ panel said "Three or four good cards together earn the country a bonus."
- **"Spotlight"**, **"Veto"** — game jargon; the one-line definitions only appear on the "YOUR POWER" screen, which goes by in 30 seconds.
- **"Clean Economy 10%"** and **"Every 1% of growth now adds 0.8 million tonnes of carbon."** — I understood; Aisyah would not.
- **"needs 5.0 a round from here"** / **"ECONOMY AVERAGE 4.2 · 0.8 short"** — what "a round" means for a growth rate is unclear even to me.
- **"Name the Funds Betting Against Us"** — Danial picked it without knowing what a "fund" is.
- **"Budget"** vs **"Company Money"** vs **"Money"** — three names for money on different screens.
- **"The Treasury"**, **"the board"**, **"Cabinet Situation Room"** — adult vocabulary.
- **"Kota Damai"**, **"Semenanjara"**, **"Aida Rahman"** — fine, but Aisyah asked if it is a real place.

## 5. Top 5 changes I would ask for

1. **Show the cards (with arrows, cost and the "Dirty card" label) during the talk**, or at least in the "I will pick…" menu. People should not be punished with "BROKE THE PROMISE" for a promise they made without seeing what they were promising.
2. **Fix "HOW TO PLAY" on the join page**, and make it a short, child-sized version (five lines with pictures of the arrows) — keep the long glossary under ⋯ for adults like me.
3. **Explain Public Trust on the screen where it is given**, and do not let a player who broke a promise take both points while the Community, whose people they are, cannot earn any. Show Aisyah's "biggest carbon cut this round" on the TV, not just on her phone.
4. **Practice should look like practice**: either do not decrement vetoes/spotlights, or write "(practice — comes back)" next to the number. And slow the practice reveal down or let the facilitator hold it until everyone has looked up.
5. **Let the talk end early when all four tap "I'm done"**, and let the Business deal say "if the Government pays half" since that is the line the game itself gave me.

## 6. Was it fun? Did it teach anything? Would I play again?

Fun: yes, more than I expected, mostly because of the family — Aisyah shouting "Explain it to me like I have to pay for it!", Danial gloating about the red X on Daddy. The "BROKE THE PROMISE" moment made all four of us laugh and argue at once, and that is what a family game should do.

Did it teach? Partly. The three-targets rule and "Promises are not enforced" are a good simple model of why climate politics is hard, and the arrows made trade-offs visible — Aisyah worked out on her own that a card with red arrows is bad for her people. But the numbers move too fast and too far to be believed (carbon 100 → 58 in one round is not science, it is fireworks), and the Public Trust result taught the opposite lesson to what I wanted the children to learn: break your promise, still get the points. I was sceptical before and I am still half-sceptical: it teaches *negotiation*, and a little about *trade-offs*; it does not really teach *how* carbon, growth and quality of life are linked.

Would I play again? Yes, once more, to finish the six rounds — but I would sit next to Aisyah, and I would want the card-blind promising fixed before I bring it to my Form 2 class.
