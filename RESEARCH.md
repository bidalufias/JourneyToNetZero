# Malaysia Context Research — verified July 2026

Every scenario in the game is anchored to a fact in this file. Nothing ships
with an invented statistic. Figures are as reported at the date given; where a
number is contested or moving, that is noted.

---

## Policy frame

**13th Malaysia Plan (13MP), 2026–2030** — tabled in Parliament 31 July 2025,
theme *"Melakar Semula Pembangunan"* (Redrawing Development). This is the
current plan; 12MP has ended.

- ~**RM430 billion** (≈USD100bn) in public and private investment earmarked
- Renewable energy to **35% of installed capacity by 2030** (from 29% in 2024)
- **Nuclear power formally reintroduced** — endorsed by Cabinet and the
  National Energy Council in November 2024, first serious consideration in over
  a decade
- **Peninsular–Sarawak grid interconnection** to expand green electricity
  exports
- **National Energy Transition Facility (NETF)** — RM2bn seed fund
- Three planetary-health strategies: climate/environment management,
  biodiversity conservation, circular economy and waste

**Net zero by 2050** — reaffirmed in 13MP, "as early as 2050."

**NDC 3.0** — submitted to UNFCCC **October 2025**. A significant shift: from a
*carbon-intensity-per-GDP* target to an **absolute** one.

- Emissions to **peak between 2029 and 2034**, with the goal of peaking by 2030
- **Up to 30 MtCO2e reduction by 2035** — 20 Mt unconditional, 10 Mt
  conditional on international finance, technology transfer and capacity
  building
- Economy-wide, all seven GHGs

**Climate Change Bill** — tabled in the first parliamentary meeting of 2026.
Establishes an independent regulatory body for compliance, data governance and
carbon reduction mechanisms. Would make Malaysia the **2nd ASEAN country** and
roughly the **60th country worldwide** with a dedicated climate law.

## Round 1 anchor — the carbon tax that keeps slipping

- Carbon tax on **iron, steel and energy** was announced in Budget 2025 for
  implementation in **2026**, at an estimated **RM35–45/tCO2e** (USD8–11)
- **April 2026: put on hold.** The Natural Resources and Environmental
  Sustainability Minister cited the **Middle East conflict** — government
  chose not to burden industry and the rakyat further given the geopolitical
  situation
- Instead of penalties first, the government would first build the framework:
  gather carbon credits, verify them, then legislate compliance
- Global oil prices rose following **Strait of Hormuz** disruption

*This is the cleanest possible opening scenario: a real, current, unresolved
policy delay caused by an external energy shock.*

## Round 2 anchor — election year and the subsidy question

**GE16 timing**

- Parliament first convened 19 December 2022 → dissolves by **December 2027**
- Election due no later than **17 February 2028**
- Analysts split across 2026 / 2027 / early 2028; **2027 considered most
  likely**
- State polls shaping the timeline: Melaka (due Feb 2027), **Sarawak (must poll
  by 10 April 2027)**, Johor (due June 2027)

**Fuel subsidy — BUDI95**

- Launched **30 September 2025**, fixing RON95 at **RM1.99/litre** for eligible
  citizens
- Quota **cut from 300 to 200 litres/month effective 1 April 2026**;
  government noted 90% of recipients already use under 200L
- Unsubsidised RON95 was **RM3.72/L** as of May 2026 (RON97 RM4.35/L)
- **T20 exclusion** under active consideration as of May 2026 — economists
  estimate savings of up to **RM1.5 billion per month**
- Government has repeatedly reaffirmed RM1.99 will hold

## Round 3 anchor — the data-centre reckoning

- Johor had approved **51 data centre projects worth USD44.3 billion** as of
  November 2025
- Pipeline plus under-construction capacity implies an **eightfold surge to
  ~7,000 MW**
- Data centres could reach **~40% of Johor's electricity demand by 2035**
- The binding constraint is **grid delivery, not generation capacity**;
  electricity and water approvals can take **up to 18 months**
- Data centres draw **nearly 200× more water** than ordinary industrial users
- Johor **rejected up to 30% of applications in 2024** on sustainability
  grounds
- Mitigation is emerging: at least one operator runs solely on treated
  wastewater and has signed a renewable deal with TNB

**Electricity tariffs (RP4, 2025–2027)** — relevant pressure on business:

- Base tariff **45.62 sen/kWh**, up **14.2%** from 39.95 sen/kWh in RP3,
  effective 1 July 2025
- TNB regulated capex for RP4 set at **RM42.82bn** (RP3: RM20.55bn)
- Commercial ICPT surcharge 3.70 sen/kWh in H1 2026; AFA swung from
  −4.99 sen/kWh (Jan 2026) to +2.59 sen/kWh (Jun 2026)

## Round 4 anchor — coal retirement and peak emissions

- Malaysia committed to **halving coal capacity by 2035** and phasing out
  **unabated coal by 2044**
- Over half of coal-fired capacity — around **7 GW** — is scheduled to retire
  between **2029 and 2033**
- **No new coal plants** to be built (stated 2024)
- RE target **40% of electricity mix by 2035**, **70% by 2050**
- Solar capacity expected to exceed **6.5 GW added between 2026 and 2029**

This round sits exactly on the NDC 3.0 peaking window (2029–2034).

## Round 5 anchor — haze emergency and the trade wall

**Haze — a live risk, not a hypothetical**

- SIIA **Haze Outlook 2026 rates the risk "red"** (high) for Brunei, Indonesia,
  Malaysia and Singapore
- Hotspots across southern ASEAN **surged 86% in H1 2026**
- Return of **El Niño**, potentially reinforced by a positive Indian Ocean
  Dipole, points to a longer, stronger dry season; severe haze expected to
  **peak August–September**
- Malaysia has proposed a **science diplomacy initiative and a sustainable
  haze fund** under the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution
  (AATHP)

**EUDR — export pressure on land use**

- EU Deforestation Regulation **enforcement from 30 December 2026**; Due
  Diligence Statements required through the EU information system from
  30 December 2025
- Requires traceability to plantation level, proving no deforestation after
  **31 December 2020**
- Roughly **12% of Malaysia's palm oil exports** go to the EU
- EU-bound volume **fell ~12% in 2025** as non-certified exporters redirected
  to India and China
- EUDR-compliant palm trades at a **USD150–220/MT premium**; certified
  exporters command 5–10% price premiums
- Penalties include market denial and fines up to **4% of EU turnover**

**CBAM** — EU carbon border mechanism moved from transitional phase
(2023–2025) to **full implementation January 2026**, covering steel,
aluminium, cement, fertiliser, electricity and hydrogen. Malaysia's own carbon
tax was partly framed as a response, so that revenue is captured domestically
rather than at the EU border.

## Round 6 anchor — the petroleum revenue cliff

- PETRONAS dividend to government for **2026: RM20 billion — down 38%** from
  RM32bn, the **lowest since 2017**
- Brent expected to average **USD60–65/bbl in 2026**, down from ~USD70
- Petroleum-related revenue contracting to **RM43bn in 2026**; non-petroleum
  revenue rising 8.1% to RM300.1bn
- Non-tax revenue down 9.9% to RM72.7bn, chiefly on lower PETRONAS dividends
- **Important correction to a common assumption:** petroleum-related revenue
  is now **17.5% of total government revenue (2025)**, down from 28% in 2022
  and a peak of **41.3% in 2009**. PETRONAS *dividends* specifically are only
  **5–6%** of 2026 revenue. Tax revenue is over 70% of the total.

The cliff is real but the game should not overstate it — the interesting
tension is that Malaysia has *already* been diversifying, and the question is
whether that continues under stress.

## Round 7 anchor — the adaptation bill

- **December 2024 Kelantan–Terengganu floods**: over **90,000 evacuees**,
  double the 2022 figure; roughly **RM2 billion** in damage
- 2025 flood losses eased 32% year-on-year, but with **higher infrastructure
  damage**
- World Bank: unchecked inundation could cut **4% of GDP by 2030**
- Worst-case modelled scenario — a **one-in-20-year flood immediately after an
  extended heatwave** — could cut **GDP by over 20% in a single year**
- Malaysia needs **USD32.56 billion** for disaster risk reduction and
  resilience
- Total climate adaptation investment could reach **USD852bn–1.13 trillion**
  nominal by 2050
- **National Adaptation Plan** launched January 2025; first full NAP targeted
  for completion in 2026. Flood resilience is now among the top criteria for
  federal project funding
- Proactive adaptation could offset **up to half** of projected losses

---

## Round 8 anchor — the last mile

Drawn from the above: nuclear entering the mix via 13MP, unabated coal gone by
2044, RE at 70% by 2050, and the hard-to-abate residual — steel, cement,
aviation, agriculture — requiring carbon capture and land sinks to reach
genuine net zero.
